## 1. Gamification in general: does it work, and for whom

- [S] Meta-analysis on gamification of learning: small positive effects on cognitive (g = .49), motivational (g = .36) and behavioural (g = .25) learning outcomes. The motivational and behavioural effects were "less stable" in high-rigour studies. Game fiction and "combining competition with collaboration" were the most effective for behavioural outcomes. Sailer & Homner, *Educational Psychology Review*, published 2019-08-15 (vol. 2020). https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w [s2-10.1007_s10648-019-09498-w.json]
- [S] Second meta-analysis on student academic performance: 30 interventions, 3,202 participants, g = 0.504 in favour of gamification. The authors open by noting the field's controversy ("exploitationware"). Bai, Hew & Huang, *Educational Research Review*, 2020-06. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2020.100322 [s2-10.1016_j.edurev.2020.100322.json]
- [S] Literature review: gamification "provides positive effects, however, the effects are greatly dependent on the context ... as well as on the users using it." Hamari, Koivisto & Sarsa, HICSS, 2014-01. https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6758978 [hamari2014.pdf]
- [S] A later review of 819 studies found results that "lean towards positive", but "the amount of mixed results is remarkable". Points, badges and leaderboards are still the most common implementation. Koivisto & Hamari, *Int. J. Information Management*, 2019-04. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijinfomgt.2018.10.013 [s2-10.1016_j.ijinfomgt.2018.10.013.json]
- [S] Self-determination theory names three innate needs, competence, autonomy and relatedness. When they are satisfied, self-motivation goes up. When they are thwarted, motivation and well-being go down. Ryan & Deci, *American Psychologist*, 2000. https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68 [openalex-10_1037_0003-066X_55_1_68.json]
- [I] The positive meta-analytic effects are small to moderate, and the motivational ones are the least robust. Treat game elements as seasoning on a working core loop (timer, plan, record), not as the engine.

## 2. Rewards and the overjustification effect (sources conflict)

- [S] A meta-analysis of 128 studies found that engagement-, completion- and performance-contingent rewards undermined free-choice intrinsic motivation (d = -0.40, -0.36, -0.28). So did all tangible rewards and all expected rewards. Positive feedback *enhanced* free-choice behaviour (d = 0.33) and self-reported interest (d = 0.31). Tangible rewards hurt children more than college students. Deci, Koestner & Ryan, *Psychological Bulletin*, 1999. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627 [deci1999.pdf]
- [S] **Conflicting source.** A meta-analysis of 96 studies concluded that "overall, reward does not decrease intrinsic motivation". Verbal praise increased it. The only negative effect came from expected tangible rewards given simply for doing a task, and that effect was "minimal". Cameron & Pierce, *Review of Educational Research*, 1994-09. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543064003363 [openalex-cameronpierce1994.json]. A direct rebuttal of Deci et al. exists (Eisenberger, Cameron & Pierce, *Psych. Bull.* 1999, https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.677). I found it but did not read its text.
- [I] Both camps agree on two points. Informational, verbal or positive feedback helps, and expected tangible rewards for merely doing a task are the risky case. For this app: celebrate *what was achieved* ("4 pomodoros on pointers, estimate was 5"), and do not pay out currency just for opening a timer.

## 3. Self-monitoring and looking at your own records

- [S] Meta-analysis of 138 RCTs (N = 19,951). Interventions that promote monitoring of goal progress improved goal attainment (d+ = 0.40, 95% CI 0.32–0.48). The effect was mediated by how often people actually monitored. It was larger "when the outcomes were reported or made public, and when the information was physically recorded." Harkin et al., *Psychological Bulletin*, 2016 (accepted manuscript). https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000025 [harkin2016.pdf]
- [S] **Counterpoint.** In six experiments, measuring an activity increased how much people did but reduced how much they enjoyed it, and lowered continued engagement after the measurement was removed. The mechanism: "by drawing attention to output, measurement can make enjoyable activities feel more like work." Etkin, *Journal of Consumer Research* 42(6), 2016 (manuscript dated 2015-11-12). https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucv095 [etkin2016.pdf]
- [S] The same paper found a moderator. "When we focused participants on what they could achieve from reading (i.e., improved learning), rather than the fun of it, measurement no longer reduced enjoyment." Measurement "may even enhance enjoyment" when the activity serves a goal. [etkin2016.pdf]
- [I] Studying C/React is goal-directed, so Etkin's cost should be small here, and Harkin's benefit applies. The risk returns if the app starts measuring things they does for fun.

## 4. Goal-gradient, endowed progress, fresh starts, implementation intentions

- [S] Goal-gradient field data: café customers bought coffee more often the closer they got to a free one. A 12-stamp card with 2 "bonus" stamps was completed faster than a 10-stamp card. A steeper acceleration predicted retention and faster re-engagement. Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng, *Journal of Marketing Research* 43, 2006-02. https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39 [kivetz2006.pdf]
- [S] Endowed progress: reframing an 8-step task as 10 steps with 2 already done raised completion and shortened completion time. The effect depends on perceived completion, not on avoiding waste. Nunes & Drèze, *Journal of Consumer Research*, 2006-03. https://doi.org/10.1086/500480 [openalex-10_1086_500480.json]
- [S] Implementation intentions ("if situation Y, then I will do X"): d = .65 on goal attainment across 94 tests. Gollwitzer & Sheeran, *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology* 38, 2006. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1 [gollwitzer2006.html, s2-10.1016_S0065-2601_06_38002-1.json]
- [S] Silverman & Barasch cite "a mental 'reset' of progress after goal failure", such as the start of a new month (Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014, the "fresh start effect"), as something that promotes subsequent goal pursuit. [silverman2023.pdf]. I did not read the primary Dai et al. abstract (it was blocked): https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901
- [I] For the design: show a visible fill toward today's pomodoro target and this week's target, because effort rises near the goal. Let them write "after dinner, at my desk, 2 pomodoros on React" when planning. Present Mondays and month starts as clean pages.

## 5. The Pomodoro Technique as Cirillo defines it

Source for this whole section: Francesco Cirillo, *The Pomodoro Technique*, v1.0 2006-10-19, v1.3 2007-06-15, CC BY-NC-ND. I read a mirrored copy of their paper at https://www.faasafety.gov/files/events/SO/SO15/2024/SO15134204/Cirillo_--_Pomodoro_Technique.pdf [cirillo-2006.pdf]. francescocirillo.com returned 404 for the URL I tried, so the current site text was not found.

- [S] "The traditional Pomodoro is 30 minutes long: 25 minutes of work plus a 5-minute break." When it rings, mark an X and take a 3–5 minute break.
- [S] "Every four Pomodoros ... take a longer break, from 15 to 30 minutes."
- [S] Interruption rule: "A Pomodoro Is Indivisible." If it is definitively interrupted, "that Pomodoro should be considered void, as if it had never been set." Interruptions are marked (apostrophe for internal, dash for external) and handled with "Inform, Negotiate, and Call back".
- [S] "If a Pomodoro Begins, It Has to Ring." Time left over after finishing a task is used for review or overlearning. You may not keep working "just for a few more minutes" past the ring.
- [S] Estimating: estimate each activity in whole pomodoros. "If It Takes More Than 5-7 Pomodoros, Break It Down." "If It Lasts Less Than One Pomodoro, Add It Up." Track over- and under-estimation.
- [S] Recording: a Records Sheet updated "at least once a day, usually at the end of the day". Recording and looking for improvements "should not take more than one Pomodoro." Recording exists for "self-observation and decision-making aimed at process improvement."
- [S] Length: the ideal pomodoro is "20 – 35 minutes long, 40 minutes at the most". The breaks at the end of a set vary with tiredness.
- [S] "Ring anxiety": the technique "mustn't be misconstrued" as external control. "There is no inspector who monitors workers' hours."
- [S] "The Next Pomodoro Will Go Better": the technique keeps attention on the current or next pomodoro, against guilt about wasted time. Cirillo says procrastinators benefit from achieving "little things".
- [S] Winding the timer is "a declaration of your determination". Marking the end and crossing off finished tasks "should use explicit gestures", so it is better if these "aren't automated."
- [S] "The Pomodoro Technique shouldn't be used for activities you do in your free time."
- [I] Several app conventions are not Cirillo's: auto-starting the next pomodoro, crediting half pomodoros, and logging a pomodoro when they walked away. A deliberate tap to start and to mark done matches their text.

## 6. Structured breaks: what studies say

- [S] Field study, 87 Dutch university students, one day of real self-study. Self-regulated breaks led to longer sessions and breaks, more fatigue and distraction, and lower concentration and motivation than systematic breaks (24 min study / 6 min break, labelled "Pomodoro", or 12/3). Invested effort and task completion did not differ, so systematic breaks reached similar completion in less time. Biwer et al., *British Journal of Educational Psychology*, 2023-03. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12593 [s2-10.1111_bjep.12593.json]
- [I] Biwer's "Pomodoro" condition was 24/6, not Cirillo's 25/3–5, and one day is a short window.
- [S] Micro-break meta-analysis (22 samples, N = 2,335): breaks boosted vigour (d = .36) and reduced fatigue (d = .35). The overall performance effect was not significant (d = .16, p = .116), and performance gains appeared only on less cognitively demanding tasks. Longer breaks gave larger performance boosts. Highly depleting tasks "may need more than 10-minute breaks." Albulescu et al., *PLOS ONE*, 2022-08-31. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0272460 [albulescu2022.html]
- [I] Sell breaks on well-being and on sustaining the session, not on "more output per minute". Programming is demanding, so the long break after a set of four deserves the full 15–30 minutes.

## 7. Streaks: what works, what backfires

Duolingo's own published numbers, all from Duolingo-authored posts:
- [S] Reaching a 7-day streak made learners 2.4× more likely to come back the next day than learners without a streak. A streak used to require meeting the daily goal, and learners with the "intense" goal were the least likely to be on a streak. Splitting the streak (1 lesson extends it) from the daily goal gave +3.3% Day-14 retention, +1% DAU and +10.5% more daily learners on a streak (relative changes), and it increased learners on a 7+ day streak by over 40%. Not everything was positive: "fewer learners were actually reaching their daily goals." Anton Yu, 2020-11-19. https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak [duo-improving-streak.html]
- [S] "Weekend Amulet" (a streak protected over the weekend): +4% more likely to return a week later, 5% less likely to lose the streak. "Streak Wager" (bet currency on 7 more days): Day-7 retention +14%. Learners who binge "were much more likely to abandon the app than learners who pace themselves". Kai Herng Loh, 2017-05-10. https://making.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals [duo-making-streaks.html]
- [S] A new streak-extension animation gave +1.7% 7-day retention for new learners. Allowing up to 2 equipped Streak Freezes gave +0.38% daily active learners. Learners with a 7-day streak were 3.6× more likely to complete their course. Duolingo says a broken streak "can have the opposite effect, and actually feel quite demotivating", and that "the fear of losing a streak could prevent learners from even attempting one". Osman Mansur, 2022-01-31. https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit/ [duo-streak.html]
- [S] Former CPO: the "streak-saver" late-night notification was the first big streak win. Overall CURR +21%, roughly a 40% cut in daily churn of the best users, and DAU 4.5× over four years. Jorge Mazal, Lenny's Newsletter, 2023-02-28. https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth [lenny-duo.html]

Peer-reviewed:
- [S] Seven studies: intact streaks shown in a behavioural log increased later engagement relative to broken ones, independent of actual past behaviour. The effect is amplified when people blame themselves for the break and weakened when they can "repair" it. Silverman & Barasch, *Journal of Consumer Research* 49(6), 2023-04. https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029 [silverman2023.pdf]
- [S] Barasch on the same work: after a break, "people will just drop off". In the language-app data, people switched to a new language rather than rebuild a streak, "because their record no longer represents them". Messaging that highlights the recent failure "typically doesn't work." CU Boulder news, 2023-04-20. https://www.colorado.edu/business/news/2023/04/20/research-streaks-marketing-tech-barasch [streaks-cu.html]
- [S] Natural experiment: GitHub removed its streak counters without warning in May 2016, keeping the graph. Afterwards long streaks were abandoned, weekend activity fell, and single-contribution days became rarer. The authors read this as counters "pushing developers to contribute on days they would have otherwise rested", and they "speculate that contributions made for the sake of a streak do not represent highly productive work." Moldon, Strohmaier & Wachs, ICSE 2021 (arXiv v3 2021-05-10). https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.02371 [github-moldon.html, github-moldon.pdf]
- [S] GitHub's own wording, 2016-05-19: "code streaks are no longer featured on your contribution graph. The simplified interface focuses on the work you're doing rather than the duration of your activity." https://github.blog/2016-05-19-more-contributions-on-your-profile/ [github-blog-2016.html]. A developer reaction welcomed the change and called streaks a "bullshit metric": TNW, 2016-05-29, https://thenextweb.com/news/github-streaks-dead-thank-the-sun-god [tnw-github.html]
- [S] Slack as "emergency reserves" (one field study, four lab studies). After failing a subgoal, people whose goals had a reserve felt more progress, felt more committed and persisted more. Sharif & Shu, *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes*, 2021. https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Sharif-Shu_EmergencyReserveFailure_OBHDP2019.pdf [sharifshu2021.pdf]. Duolingo cites a "University of Pennsylvania and UCLA" slack study as the basis for Streak Freeze [duo-streak.html]. [I] That is very likely this work.
- [S] Qualitative study (9 years of forum posts plus 15 interviews): Duolingo users "too fixated on gamification" lose learning time. Named causes include "competitiveness, overindulgence in playfulness, and herding". Hadi Mogavi et al., arXiv 2022-03-30. https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16175 [duo-misuse-arxiv.html]
- [I] Streak anxiety and guilt as a population-level harm is mostly anecdote. The measured harms are behavioural: quitting after a break, rest days lost, and low-value "keep the streak" actions.
- [I] Duolingo's numbers measure retention for a business. A lift of +0.38% DAU says nothing about the learner's well-being.

## 8. Habit-app mechanics with documented behaviour

- [S] Habitica study (one-user IPA, then 45 users over 2 weeks): every participant experienced counterproductive effects. The most prevalent was "being punished by Habitica in especially productive times, since one does not manage to check off tasks in time." People also relabelled tasks to dodge punishment. The prevalence of these effects correlated with perceiving the reward system as inappropriate, and it predicted motivation change over time. Diefenbach & Müssig, *Int. J. Human-Computer Studies* 127, 2019. https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/77668 [habitica-lmu.html]
- [S] Todoist Karma: points for adding tasks, completing them on time, reaching daily/weekly goals and keeping streaks. Points are *lost* for tasks 5+ days overdue. Eight levels, Beginner to Enlightened. Forgiveness features: days off per weekday, Vacation mode, goals can be turned off (set to 0), "goal celebrations" can be toggled, and support restores a broken streak up to 3 times per account. Todoist Help, updated 2026-11-13. https://todoist.com/help/articles/introduction-to-karma-OgWkWy [todoist-karma2.html]
- [S] TickTick focus statistics: pomos earned, focused hours, "Most Focused Moments", completion rate of focus goals, day-on-day and week-on-week comparisons, time by list and tag. TickTick blog, 2020-05-06. https://blog.ticktick.com/2020/05/06/brand-new-focus-experience-ticktick/ [ticktick-2020-focus.html]. "Win Badges" was announced as upcoming on 2020-06-11, https://blog.ticktick.com/2020/06/11/review-of-recent-updates-in-ticktick/ [ticktick-2020-updates.html]. The achievement-score and level rules were not found in a page I could read.
- [S] Forest: a tree grows during focus and "dies if you switch to another app mid-session". Completed sessions form "a visible forest". Forest describes this as tapping "loss aversion". **Plant Together**: friends join a room by code and plant the same tree, and "if one person gives up, everyone's tree dies." Real trees: through Trees for the Future, "over 2 million real trees ... since 2014" (company claim; per-tree GPS verification "isn't available"). Optional pauses of up to 5 minutes without killing the tree. forestapp.cc/llms-full.txt, undated, fetched 2026-11-24. https://forestapp.cc/llms-full.txt [forest-llms.txt]
- [S] Finch: no peer-reviewed outcome study found.
- [I] Habitica shows how punishment can backfire, and Forest's shared-death room applies a similar mechanic to the friend group. For one procrastinating student and one friend, a shared tree that dies because of the other person risks blame. A "we finished together" record carries no such risk.

## 9. Social mechanics for two to a few friends

- [S] Social facilitation meta-analysis (241 studies, about 24,000 participants). The presence of others explains only 0.3–3% of variance. It speeds simple tasks, slows complex tasks and impairs accuracy on complex tasks. The effects are "surprisingly unrelated" to evaluation apprehension. Bond & Titus, *Psychological Bulletin*, 1983-09. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.94.2.265 [openalex-10_1037_0033-2909_94_2_265.json]
- [I] These are lab co-action studies, not co-working on separate tasks. They warn against live "watching" while they debug something hard, and they do not argue against a shared session they joins voluntarily.
- [S] Body doubling, EEG study, N = 26 (ADHD and controls): no statistically significant difference between body doubling and working alone, no consistent negative effects, and "promising trends for unmedicated ADHD participants." The authors note "little empirical evidence supports its use." Proc. ASSETS '25, 2025-10-22. https://doi.org/10.1145/3663547.3759743 [s2b-10_1145_3663547_3759743.json]
- [S] Focusmate's own survey of 842 active members reported a 143% average productivity increase, and 96% said it helped them follow through. This is a company survey with no visible control group. Focusmate Media Kit, undated, fetched 2026-11-24. https://www.focusmate.com/?p=3865 [focusmate-mediakit.html]
- [S] Duolingo Friend Streak (up to 5 friends): learners with at least one Friend Streak are "22% more likely to complete their daily lesson" (correlational). The PM described it as "the first social feature ... that doesn't require you to actually do anything with another learner ... like studying next to a friend at the library." Jackson Shuttleworth, 2024-09-20. https://blog.duolingo.com/product-lessons-friend-streak/ [duo-friend-streak-lessons.html]
- [S] Strava kudos (5 Dutch clubs, 329 members, 11 monthly waves, SIENA models): "receiving kudos induced runners to run more and more often." People converged on the behaviour of the friends they gave kudos to, and more so toward friends who ran *less*. Franken, Bekhuis & Tolsma, *Social Networks*, online 2022-10-13 (issue 2023-01). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socnet.2022.10.001 [openalex-10_1016_j_socnet_2022_10_001.json]. Press summary: athletes with kudos turned off ran less. In 4 of 5 clubs, peer influence mattered more than kudos received. Canadian Running, 2023-01-10, https://runningmagazine.ca/?p=91520 [strava-runmag.html]
- [S] Exercise contagion (about 1.1M runners, 5 years, weather as a natural experiment): exercise spreads through friendships. "Less active runners influence more active runners, but not the reverse." Aral & Nicolaides, *Nature Communications*, 2017-04-18. https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms14753 [aral2017.html]
- [S] STEP UP RCT (602 adults, 24 weeks plus 12 weeks follow-up). Step increase vs control: competition +920/day, support +689, collaboration +637. During follow-up, only competition stayed significantly above control (+569). Patel et al., *JAMA Internal Medicine*, 2019-12-01. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.3505 [pubmed-efetch-bondtitus-patel.txt, s2-10.1001_jamainternmed.2019.3505.json]

Leaderboards (sources conflict):
- [S] Duolingo's leagues ranked users against *strangers of similar engagement*, not friends, on the stated hypothesis that "closeness of the competitor's engagement would be more important than the closeness of personal relationships". Learning time rose 17%, and highly engaged learners tripled. Mazal, 2023-02-28 [lenny-duo.html]
- [S] Against: a gamified course with a leaderboard, badges and coins (71 students, 4 time points) showed "less motivation, satisfaction, and empowerment over time" and lower final-exam scores, mediated by intrinsic motivation. Hanus & Fox, *Computers & Education* 80, 2015. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2014.08.019. Weak copy: I could only access the Semantic Scholar summary of the abstract [hanusfox-updf.html, s2b-10_1016_j_compedu_2014_08_019.json]. The publisher returned 403, and a 2018 corrigendum exists that I did not read.
- [S] Against: a review of leaderboard research says those in lower ranks "tend not to respond to the leaderboard and are likely to feel inadequate". On a macro leaderboard, repeated low rank accumulates failure experiences. The authors recommend pairing a macro board with "micro leaderboards". Park & Kim, *JMIR Serious Games*, 2021-04-20. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8097522/ [leaderboard-pmc8097522.html]
- [S] Temporal versus social comparison (RCT, 583 children aged 8–16): favourable comparisons with others and with one's past self both raised pride. Only the comparison with one's past self did so "without triggering superiority goals", and it gave "a sense of progress and insight." Gürel et al., *J. Experimental Psychology: General* 149(8), 2020. https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000733 [gurel2020.pdf]
- [I] How the conflict resolves: competition works when the field is matched (Duolingo leagues), for people who opt in (STEP UP participants chose a game), and when it is mixed with collaboration (Sailer). It hurts when the ranking is absolute and the same people sit at the bottom (Hanus & Fox, Park & Kim). With two friends of unequal pace, a ranked board guarantees one permanent loser. Aral, and Franken et al., also suggest the faster friend drifts toward the slower one.

## 10. Tasteful reward moments with documented handling

- [S] Duolingo: streak-extension animation, with special ones on milestone days, +1.7% D7 retention for new learners (2022-01-31) [duo-streak.html]
- [S] Todoist: "goal celebrations" when the daily or weekly goal is hit, switchable in settings. The Karma Trend line graph shows the past week. Reaching "Enlightened" unlocks a theme. (2026-11-13) [todoist-karma2.html]
- [S] Asana: "occasional animated commemoration of a job well done" (unicorn, narwhal, phoenix, yeti). The celebrations are opt-in from Display settings. The yeti appears on "the deepest, oldest, hardest-to-complete tasks". Asana blog, 2016-03 (yeti post, undated in the copy). https://asana.com/inside-asana/new-celebrations-meet-the-yeti [asana-yeti.md]
- [S] GitHub: a contribution graph with no streak counter, framed as "the work you're doing rather than the duration" (2016-05-19) [github-blog-2016.html]
- [S] Forest: the session result is a tree added to a visible forest, a "living record of your focused time" [forest-llms.txt]
- [S] Peer-reviewed evidence on where "rewarding" turns "childish": not found. The closest evidence is age effects. Tangible rewards are more detrimental for children, and verbal rewards are less enhancing for them (Deci et al. 1999) [deci1999.pdf]
- [I] The line in practice: a reward is tasteful when it reports something true (finished, beat your estimate, new personal best), happens rarely, is brief, and can be turned off. It turns childish when it is constant, decorative, and unrelated to the work.

## Design principles (all [I], inferred from the above)
1. **The core loop is Cirillo's.** Use a deliberate start, 25/5 with a 15–30 min break after four, interrupted means void (never shamed), estimates in whole pomodoros, "break it down" above 5–7, and an end-of-day record that takes at most one pomodoro.
2. **"You vs past you" is the main progress view.** Use a calendar heatmap of pomodoros, this week vs last week, and estimate accuracy over time (Harkin, Gürel, Cirillo §2.1.5). Avoid "you vs your friend" rankings.
3. **Streaks, if kept, are forgiving by design.** Count consistency at a low bar (1 pomodoro, as in Duolingo 2020). Include rest days and a few built-in reserves (Sharif & Shu, Weekend Amulet, Todoist days off). After a break, show the total kept ("41 of the last 45 days") rather than a reset to zero (Silverman & Barasch, Barasch's quote). Never charge money or ads to repair a streak.
4. **No punishment mechanics.** Do not take points away and do not let an object die because of someone else (Habitica, and the Plant Together risk). A voided pomodoro simply does not count.
5. **For the friend group, presence and cooperation, not rank.** Offer a shared live session ("studying next to a friend at the library"), a shared weekly total or goal that both fill, and one-tap kudos on a finished session or day. Make the ranked board absent or opt-in.
6. **Rewards report achievements, not activity.** Give a short, rare, switch-off-able moment on real milestones: set of four done, task finished within estimate, new personal best week, weekly target met. Do not give currency for merely starting (Deci et al. 1999). Celebrations stay restrained (Asana and Todoist are opt-in or toggleable).
7. **Frame tracking around learning.** "What you learned: 6 pomodoros on React hooks" keeps measurement from feeling like work (Etkin's moderator). Tag pomodoros by subject so the record reads as a learning log.
