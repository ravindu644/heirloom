Today, lap by lap: three ways to bring in the lane

Pick B2. It brings the lane in without a second copy of the day: each lap is drawn once, on its own row, against the day's hours, and read down the slivers make the same lane you sent.

  • All three keep B: one list in the order the day ran, each lap adding its minutes to "Day so far", and the last split is the figure at the top.
  • The lap from 23:46 to 00:31 adds its 31 minutes after midnight. Its row keeps its real times and says the first 14 count on Friday, so 31 and 16 make 47.
  • A lap not counted keeps its place, hollow, with no split. "Don't count it" and "Count it again" stand under the lap's words, as in B, 48px to a finger.
  • A full day shows its latest 5 laps; "3 earlier laps, 1h 15m" opens the rest in place, and the splits under it start from that 1h 15m.
B1

The lane on top, as the list's map.

The day's lane sits under the figure. A tap on it picks the nearest lap, brings that lap's row into view and marks it (opening the earlier laps first if it is one of them).

How it avoids showing the day twice: the lane holds the shapes and the rows hold the words and splits. The rows lose their squares and their break lines, so nothing is drawn in both places.

Gives up: it is still two places to look at for one day. A block is too small for a finger, so the whole lane is one 48px target that picks the nearest lap, and a stranger may not guess it can be tapped.

B2

The lane cut into the rows.The pick

Each lap's row carries a sliver of the day's hours with only its own lap on it and the break that followed. Read down, the slivers step to the right and make the lane. The hours are named once, over the column.

How it avoids showing the day twice: there is no separate lane. The picture is made of the rows, one lap per row, and the breaks live only on the slivers.

Gives up: each row is a line taller on a phone, and the whole day's shape shows only when the earlier laps are open.

B3

A bar of the total under the figure.

Under 47m, one bar cut into a segment per lap that counted, each as long as the minutes it gave today, the way Today draws the day's bar. The last split is the bar's end.

How it avoids showing the day twice: the bar is the figure drawn, not the day. It has no hours and no breaks; the rows say when, and they lose their squares.

Gives up: there is no hour lane, so the shape of the day is only in the rows' times. Of the three, it is the furthest from the picture the user sent.
