Signing in on a wide screen

Three ways to compose the browser’s sign-in from 720px up. The form’s words and controls are as built; only where things stand, their sizes and the drawing’s scale change. Signed out there are no destinations, so in all three the empty sidebar goes and the mark stands alone at the top left, where it stands on every screen. A phone keeps today’s page: the form, then the drawing and its words under it.

S1

Two halves, the user’s idea: the welcome on the ground at the left, the form on a surface that runs the right half’s full height. The mark, the drawing’s top and the form’s heading share one top line. My pick

What it gives up: It is the shape most sign-in pages have. The surface is a second tone the rest of the app never uses for a whole half of a screen.

S2

The drawing is the field. Seven weeks as tall as the page, and one card at the right that holds the title, the sentence and the form, lying over the drawing’s edge where the window is narrow.

What it gives up: The drawing becomes a backdrop rather than a picture to read, and the card overlaps it with no shadow, which DESIGN.md keeps for what floats. On a short window the squares shrink with the height.

S3

One column down the middle. A masthead of the drawing, small, beside the title and sentence; the form under it on the same left edge.

What it gives up: The drawing is at its smallest (24px squares), and on a 1440 screen two wide bands of ground stand empty at the sides.
