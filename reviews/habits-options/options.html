Habits: three ways to tick a round

My pick is A. Every time a habit is done in a day gets its own box that ticks when you tap it, with its reminder time under it, and nothing changes in how check-ins are stored or synced. B is the most literal answer to "tick the 08:00 round" but splits one habit across the list and needs a new field on every check-in; C copes best with 20 a day but gives up the tick you asked for.

In all three: what replaces "1 day running"

6 days in a row
The days it was planned and done, one after another. A rest day, the green ring on its week, does not break the row: two a week may stay empty. The room already says it this way.
9 of the last 14 days
After a day missed past those two, the days kept instead, never a zero. "Planned" is said only for a habit that is not every day: "11 of the last 14 planned days". A day not over yet is never counted as missed.
Nothing
While the row is 1. A filled square on the week already says it was done; "1 day running" said it again in words nobody could place.

A

My pickThe safe one A box for each time a day

The logic. A tap on an empty box ticks it (the next one from the left, as the boxes fill in order); a tap on a ticked box takes the last tick back. A reminder time stands under its box.

The cost. Ticks fill in order, since a reminder time belongs to the 1st, 2nd or 3rd check-in, not to a slot: tap the 16:00 box first and the 08:00 one ticks. A habit done 8 times a day takes two lines of boxes, 20 takes three.

B

The day by the clock

The logic. A time a day with a reminder stands in the list at its hour, as a row of its own; a tap ticks that one and only that one, early or late, and a tap on a ticked one takes back just it. A time with no reminder is a row too, under "No time set"; each habit's week moves under both.

The cost. A check-in has to say which time it was: one new field, checkin.round (1 to target; a check-in without one fills the lowest empty time, and undo sets deleted on that time's check-in). One habit stands in as many places as it has times (20 glasses of water is 20 rows), and the day and the weeks are two lists to scroll on a phone.

C

Count up, do not tick

The logic. Done once a day keeps its box. Done more often gets the kit's stepper: plus checks in once and the figure steps up, minus takes the last one back. A box is for yes or no; a count gets the control that adds one.

The cost. There is no tick on a counted habit at all, which is what you asked for, and a reminder time shows only as the next one due. In return 20 a day is one line, and taking one back is always in sight, not only for the ten seconds of an Undo.
